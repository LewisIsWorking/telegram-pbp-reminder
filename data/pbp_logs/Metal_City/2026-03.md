And now here.

Chik's eyes snapped open.

A door he couldn't see from this angle. And somewhere nearby, the aftermath of violence: the sharp smell of discharged energy weapons, the copper tang of blood, the distinctive ozone burn of a rifle shot fired in close quarters.

The noises outside had just... stopped.

He lay still for three full breaths, the way Grandmother Niska had taught him. Assess before moving. Let the situation come to you.

The force needle pulsed against his skin. Here, it seemed to say. I'm here. We're here.

His rifle, wherever it was, screamed in the silence of his mind. Come find me. Come find me. I'm not whole without you.

Chik's whiskers twitched.

Yeah. Yeah, I know, buddy. Working on it.

He pushed himself up, movements quick and economical despite the lingering grogginess. The cell was basic: a sleeping shelf bolted to the wall, a sanitary unit that had seen better centuries, and absolutely nothing else. No personal effects. No gear. Just him and the clothes on his back and the needle hidden against his skin.

And the new... whatever it was. The warmth. The presence that hadn't been there before the Scream.

He crossed to the door in three quick steps, pressed his face close to the gap, and listened.

Voices. Quiet. Not the rough cadence of pirates—something else. Cautious. Coordinated. The kind of quiet that meant people were planning, not panicking. The kind of quiet that meant the pirates might be having a very bad day. Chik's tail gave an involuntary flick of satisfaction.

He rapped his knuckles against the door—three quick taps, a pause, two more. An old creche signal. Anyone there? Friend or foe?

"Hey," he called out, keeping his voice low but not quite a whisper. "Hey! Out there! Little rat guy in a cell, no weapons, very confused. If whoever just made that mess is friendly, I could really use a clue about why I'm in here and whether I should be panicking."

"Oh, and if anyone's seen my rifle—Magnetar, matte black, copper coils, has a little rat-chewing-chains sticker on the stock—please tell me it's not in pirate hands. I just got it to stop making that weird clicking noise and I will be very annoyed if I have to start over."

He leaned his forehead against the cool metal of the door, whiskers pressed flat. Quick-Paw, if you're listening, he thought, I could really use some of that fire right about now. Just saying.

Outside, the silence held. Inside, the needle waited. And somewhere on this ship, his rifle screamed to be found.
